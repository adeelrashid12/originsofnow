Plastics
WHERE DOES ALL THAT PLASTIC GO?
A bottle may be used for minutes. But the material doesn't simply disappear when we throw it away.
Globally, plastic waste is landfilled, incinerated, recycled or mismanaged. UNEP reports that 22% of plastic waste is mismanaged and becomes litter, while plastic packaging represents a major source of single-use plastic waste.
Plastic pollution can enter land, rivers and oceans, affecting ecosystems and wildlife. UNEP estimates that millions of tonnes of plastic enter aquatic environments each year.
AND WHAT ABOUT US?
As larger pieces of plastic break down, they can form microplastics and nanoplastics.
Researchers have reported micro- and nanoplastics in human biological samples including blood, lungs and placenta. But an important question remains: What does this mean for long-term human health?
Scientists are still working to answer that.
WHO describes microplastic exposure as an emerging area of concern and highlights significant gaps in our understanding of potential health effects, particularly for the smallest particles.
 Read what the science says:
NIEHS — What we know about microplastics and nanoplastics — useful overview of research finding these particles in human tissues and the remaining uncertainty about health effects. 
UNEP — Everything you need to know about plastic pollution — excellent source for the environmental/waste side of the story. 
UNEP — Riverine plastic pollution — specifically useful for showing how mismanaged plastic reaches rivers and aquatic environments.
